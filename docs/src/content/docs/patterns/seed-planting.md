---
title: "Seed Planting"
description: "Letting the audience arrive at the conclusion themselves."
---

*Don't impose the idea. Let them discover it.*

## The Pattern

Instead of advocating directly for a conclusion:
- Present observations the audience will recognize
- Ask questions that lead naturally to the insight
- Let them feel ownership of the answer

## When to Use

- Persuasion contexts where direct advocacy triggers resistance
- Building buy-in for changes that require adoption
- When the audience needs to internalize, not just hear
- Situations where "you're right" matters more than "I told you"

## Implementation Notes

- Start with shared observations, not assertions
- Questions should be genuine, not rhetorical traps
- The conclusion should feel inevitable by the time you reach it
- Leave room for the audience to articulate it first

## Structure

1. **Shared context**: "We've all noticed..."
2. **Uncomfortable observations**: Things that don't quite fit
3. **Questions that focus attention**: "What would that mean for...?"
4. **The landing**: Let them say it, or say it as confirmation of what's now obvious

## Anti-patterns

- Questions that are obviously leading ("Don't you think we should...?")
- Rushing to the conclusion before the ground is prepared
- Pretending to ask when you're really telling
- Using this pattern when direct communication would be more respectful

## When NOT to Use

- Urgent situations requiring immediate action
- Audiences who prefer direct communication
- When you don't actually want input, just compliance
